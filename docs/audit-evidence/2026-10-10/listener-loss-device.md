Doctor Biz: the reason-counter image directly reproduced one capacity-full publication rejection: dropped=1, dropped_full=1, dropped_contended=0, dropped_poisoned=0, sticky loss=true. Every complete receipt validates with production listener_export_reasons. Counters are separate atomic loads, so this report does not require their sum to equal the aggregate. Strict loss acceptance correctly fails; this finding attributes the new run only and does not identify the historical lost record.

Five complete UART receipts show record size 248 and queue size 2024 bytes. The first join was exported with zero drops at 24662674 us. The next receipt, export 58499335 us, first reports the sticky full loss while exporting second join and timeout records. Its event-time range does not timestamp the loss itself.

The full run retains nine event records: three Joined, one Timeout, three Departure and two Closed. Departure renders several UART lines but is one queue record. Nine exported records plus aggregate dropped=1 establish at least ten publication attempts, at least nine after the first join's completed export batch. The final exported batch contains two records; no queue-empty receipt proves all accepted records were drained. Additional pending records and total attempts remain unknown. The lost payload remains unknown; absent close text is insufficient to identify it. Dequeue timing and peak occupancy are unrecorded, but dropped_full directly proves storage held eight records when one publication acquired the mutex. It does not show why the exporter fell behind.

Parent ruling: keep capacity eight while isolating encoder/exporter behavior. No retention change is approved. The observed full rejection explains this run's strict evidence failure, but does not establish the delay cause. Nine exported records and one measured loss do not bound the whole run's record set because final pending records are unknown; therefore neither nine nor ten slots can be claimed to cover the complete run from this evidence. Any future capacity proposal needs an explicit finite event bound or final drain evidence, measured layout/stack costs, and a matched repeat. Preserve sticky-loss rejection and this failed run regardless of later changes.
