The runner checks that aplay is alive after capture. Its read_header error appears after the trial and before completed True, compatible with termination during cleanup. No timestamped proof isolates that error or proves continuous physical source audio; process liveness is the supported claim.

Pinned IDF 5.5.5 `api_lib.c:1067` calls netconn_apimsg for nonblocking TCP writes too; `tcpip.c:460–461` posts a mailbox message and waits on a completion semaphore with core locking disabled. Generated configuration has TCP/IP priority 18, no affinity, 100 Hz ticks. MP3 encoder priority 8 and listener priority 7 share core 1; capture is priority 11. Encoder yields one tick after eight MP3 frames (192 ms audio). A resumed listener can therefore wait below the encoder after the TCP/IP completion signal. Sparse long sends and high encoder runtime support this hypothesis but do not prove it: mailbox wait, stack work, interrupts and post-signal scheduling remain combined in send wall.

A bounded direct mailbox measurement would separate post time, dispatch start, completion signal and caller resume, but needs maintaining an IDF patch. The smallest controlled application experiment is only LISTENER_THREAD priority 7→9, leaving accept priority 7.
